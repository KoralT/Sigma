# Operational Repositories

> **Product Positioning**
>
> Operational Repositories are Sigma's organizational knowledge product.
>
> They enable organizations to capture, organize, govern, discover, and continuously evolve operational knowledge so that information becomes trusted organizational assets rather than isolated documents or disconnected data sources.
>
> Operational Repositories establish the persistent knowledge foundation consumed by Commander Space and the Sigma Platform.

---

# Purpose

Operational Repositories provide the organizational knowledge layer of the Sigma ecosystem.

Their purpose is to transform operational information into structured, governed, reusable organizational assets that continuously support planning, execution, decision-making, and organizational learning.

Rather than functioning as traditional document repositories or file management systems, Operational Repositories organize knowledge around operational meaning.

Every operational asset becomes part of a continuously evolving organizational knowledge graph that can be discovered, related, governed, and reused across future operational activities.

The product ensures that organizational knowledge remains available independently of individual contributors while continuously strengthening the organization's operational understanding.

# Part I – Repository Vision

## Organizational Knowledge as an Operational Capability

Operational knowledge represents one of the organization's most valuable assets.

However, knowledge frequently exists as disconnected documents, isolated databases, personal experience, informal communication, and operational artifacts distributed across multiple systems.

Operational Repositories transform these fragmented sources into one continuously evolving organizational knowledge environment.

Knowledge becomes structured, connected, governed, and continuously reusable.

---

## From Documents to Operational Assets

Traditional repositories primarily manage files.

Operational Repositories manage operational assets.

Every operational asset possesses meaning, ownership, relationships, lifecycle, operational context, and organizational relevance.

Knowledge is therefore no longer organized according to storage location.

It is organized according to operational value.

---

## Knowledge Supporting Operations

Operational Repositories do not exist to preserve information.

They exist to strengthen operational execution.

Every operational asset should contribute to:

- planning,
- readiness,
- decision-making,
- execution,
- organizational learning,
- future operational activities.

Knowledge becomes operational infrastructure rather than historical documentation.

---

## Relationship with Commander Space

Operational Repositories provide the persistent knowledge foundation consumed throughout Commander Space.

Headquarters Workspace uses repositories to support organizational coordination.

Personal Workspace uses repositories to support preparation and execution.

Neither workspace owns organizational knowledge directly.

Both continuously consume and enrich Operational Repositories through shared Sigma capabilities.

Operational knowledge therefore remains consistent throughout the organization while continuously evolving through operational activity.

---

## Repository Principles

The design of Operational Repositories follows several guiding principles.

### Knowledge Before Documents

The repository manages operational knowledge rather than files.

---

### Relationships Before Hierarchies

Operational assets derive meaning through relationships rather than folder structures.

---

### Continuous Evolution

Knowledge continuously evolves together with operational activity.

---

### Organizational Ownership

Knowledge belongs to the organization rather than to individual contributors.

---

### Reuse Before Recreation

Existing organizational knowledge should always be discoverable before new knowledge is created.

# Part III – Operational Assets

Operational Assets are the fundamental building blocks of Operational Repositories.

Every piece of organizational knowledge managed by Sigma is represented as an operational asset.

Unlike traditional documents or database records, operational assets represent meaningful organizational knowledge that exists within an operational context, participates in organizational relationships, and continuously evolves throughout its lifecycle.

Operational Assets establish a common language for representing organizational knowledge across the Sigma ecosystem.

Organizations generate many forms of knowledge.

Maps.

Orders.

Lessons learned.

Evidence.

Reference material.

Traditionally, these artifacts are managed independently.

Operational Repositories instead represent them through a common operational model.

Regardless of their original format, every artifact becomes an Operational Asset.

This unified representation enables knowledge to be governed, connected, discovered, and reused consistently across the organization.

## Characteristics of an Operational Asset

Every Operational Asset possesses a shared set of characteristics that define its role within the organizational knowledge ecosystem.

An asset typically includes:

- operational purpose,
- ownership,
- operational status,
- lifecycle state,
- provenance,
- relationships,
- operational context,
- supporting metadata,
- governance information.

These characteristics allow assets to participate in the organization's broader operational understanding rather than existing as isolated information objects.

## Assets Represent Organizational Meaning

Operational Assets are not defined by file type.

They are defined by operational meaning.

For example, a map, a document, an image, a checklist, or a sensor report may all represent operational assets if they contribute meaningful organizational knowledge.

The repository therefore classifies assets according to what they represent, not how they are stored.

This distinction enables a consistent knowledge model independent of technology or content format.

## Assets Exist Within Context

An asset may support:

- an operational objective,
- a mission,
- an activity,
- a decision,
- a location,
- an organizational unit,
- a person,
- another operational asset.

Operational knowledge changes over time.

Procedures are updated.

Plans evolve.

Evidence accumulates.

Operational understanding improves.

Rather than treating updates as replacements, Operational Repositories preserve the evolution of knowledge throughout its lifecycle.

Every asset therefore represents both its current state and its operational history.

## Assets Contribute to Trusted Context

Operational Assets collectively establish the Trusted Context consumed throughout Sigma.

Each individual asset contributes only a portion of organizational understanding.

Meaning emerges when assets are interpreted together through their relationships, metadata, governance, and operational context.

Trusted Context is therefore not stored directly.

# Part VIII – Knowledge Consumption

Operational knowledge creates value only when it is actively consumed to improve organizational outcomes.

Operational Repositories therefore focus not only on storing and governing knowledge, but also on enabling its continuous use throughout operational planning, execution, learning, and decision-making.

Knowledge Consumption describes how trusted organizational knowledge becomes an active operational capability across the Sigma ecosystem.

The repository is not an archive.

It is a continuously utilized organizational resource.

---

# Knowledge Supporting Operations

Operational knowledge should be available whenever organizational understanding is required.

Rather than requiring users to search for information manually, Operational Repositories continuously provide relevant knowledge at the point of operational need.

Knowledge consumption therefore becomes embedded within operational workflows instead of existing as a separate activity.

The objective is to minimize the distance between organizational knowledge and operational action.

---

## Human Consumption

Operational personnel consume knowledge to understand situations, prepare for responsibilities, execute activities, and make informed decisions.

Knowledge may support:

- operational planning,
- mission preparation,
- readiness assessments,
- operational coordination,
- decision-making,
- organizational learning,
- after-action review.

Operational Repositories ensure that users receive trusted knowledge within the context of their current operational responsibilities.

Knowledge becomes immediately applicable rather than merely accessible.

---

## Leadership Consumption

Organizational leadership consumes Operational Repositories to strengthen strategic and operational coordination.

Leaders rely on organizational knowledge to:

- understand operational readiness,
- evaluate organizational progress,
- review historical experience,
- examine supporting evidence,
- understand operational dependencies,
- identify organizational risks,
- support executive decision-making.

Leadership therefore consumes synthesized organizational understanding rather than isolated operational documents.

---

## Operational Process Consumption

Operational knowledge also supports organizational processes.

Planning activities.

Approval workflows.

Readiness evaluations.

Operational reviews.

Governance processes.

Lessons learned.

Operational Repositories continuously provide trusted knowledge throughout these organizational processes, ensuring consistency across repeated operational activities.

Knowledge therefore becomes part of organizational execution rather than external documentation.

---

## AI Consumption

Artificial intelligence consumes Operational Repositories as its primary organizational knowledge source.

Rather than relying upon isolated prompts or external information, AI continuously interprets trusted Operational Assets together with their metadata, relationships, governance status, and operational context.

AI capabilities may consume repositories to:

- summarize operational situations,
- explain organizational context,
- recommend supporting knowledge,
- identify operational dependencies,
- assist preparation,
- strengthen decision support,
- improve organizational awareness.

Importantly, AI consumes knowledge.

It does not replace it.

The repository remains the authoritative organizational source.

---

## Contextual Knowledge Delivery

Knowledge should appear when it becomes operationally relevant.

Operational Repositories continuously deliver knowledge according to context rather than user requests alone.

Context may include:

- current operational responsibilities,
- organizational objectives,
- readiness state,
- operational phase,
- recent organizational changes,
- related operational activities,
- geographic context.

Users receive knowledge that supports their current work without needing to actively search for every asset.

---

## Reuse Across Operations

Operational knowledge should create increasing organizational value over time.

Knowledge generated during one operation should strengthen future planning, preparation, execution, and learning.

Operational Repositories therefore encourage continuous reuse of:

- operational guidance,
- validated procedures,
- historical experience,
- supporting evidence,
- organizational lessons,
- proven operational practices.

Every operational activity enriches the organization's future capabilities.

Knowledge compounds rather than expires.

---

## Continuous Organizational Learning

Every operational activity produces new organizational understanding.

As this understanding is captured within Operational Repositories, it immediately becomes available to future users, future operations, and future organizational decisions.

Learning therefore becomes a continuous organizational capability rather than an isolated retrospective exercise.

Operational experience remains permanently available regardless of organizational change.

---

## Relationship with Commander Space

Commander Space serves as the primary experience through which Operational Repositories are consumed.

Headquarters Workspace consumes organizational knowledge to coordinate operations and support leadership decisions.

Personal Workspace consumes organizational knowledge to prepare individuals, strengthen execution, and maintain operational awareness.

Both workspaces rely on the same trusted repository while presenting knowledge according to their respective operational perspectives.

The repository provides knowledge.

Commander Space transforms that knowledge into operational experience.

---

# Repository Evolution

Operational Repositories are designed to evolve continuously as organizational knowledge, operational practices, and technology mature.

Future evolution should strengthen the organization's ability to generate, govern, discover, connect, and apply knowledge without increasing operational complexity.

Every enhancement should reinforce the repository's role as the trusted knowledge foundation of the Sigma ecosystem.

---

## Evolution Principles

Future development should remain aligned with several enduring principles.

### Increase Knowledge Quality

Organizational knowledge should become progressively more accurate, connected, and trustworthy over time.

---

### Reduce Knowledge Friction

Creating, discovering, governing, and consuming knowledge should require progressively less effort from operational personnel.

---

### Strengthen Organizational Memory

Future capabilities should improve the organization's ability to preserve and transfer operational understanding across personnel, organizational changes, and operational generations.

---

### Expand Contextual Intelligence

Operational Repositories should continuously improve their ability to connect knowledge, identify relationships, and deliver contextually relevant organizational understanding.

---

### Support Responsible AI

Future AI capabilities should continue relying upon transparent, governed, and explainable organizational knowledge rather than opaque reasoning or external assumptions.

Operational Repositories remain the trusted knowledge foundation upon which intelligent capabilities evolve.

---

# Summary

Operational Repositories are Sigma's organizational knowledge product.

They transform operational information into governed, connected, discoverable, and continuously reusable Operational Assets.

By managing assets, metadata, relationships, governance, and contextual discovery as a unified knowledge model, the repositories establish the Trusted Context consumed throughout the Sigma ecosystem.

Commander Space relies on Operational Repositories to deliver meaningful operational experiences.

The Sigma Platform enriches and manages the underlying knowledge model.

Together they ensure that organizational knowledge remains a living operational capability—continuously supporting planning, readiness, execution, decision-making, and organizational learning across every level of the organization.
